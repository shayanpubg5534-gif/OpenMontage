# The Epoch Ledger: visual language (a reference, not a restriction)

What our recurring visual choices mean, so the series reads as one voice across episodes. Break any of these when a
subject is better served, and say why in the treatment.

## Identity
- **Ink on warm paper.**
  - Hand-drawn Open Peeps characters (the artist's line work, never distorted) in painted, cinematic sets.
  - Documents, maps and charts are printed on paper of the same warmth.
  - The world of the film and its explanations share one surface.
- **The ledger line.** A ruled line (an ink rule or a column divider) is our connective motif. It can draw a document,
  become a chart's axis, carry a timeline, or split a balance sheet into two sides.

## Colour meaning (from `config/production.json`; consistent within an episode)

| Colour | Means |
|---|---|
| **Gold** | The figure or word that matters now (captions, the key number). Not decoration. |
| **Ink navy / charcoal** | Institutions, the system, the record |
| **Green** | Money arriving, paid, healthy |
| **Red** | Loss, default, overdue, crisis. Used sparingly so it still alarms. |
| **Warm practical light** (lamps, low sun) | People, homes, hope, the human stakes |
| **Cool, even light** | Institutions, processes, distance from people |
| **Hard contrast, falling light** | Crisis, consequence |

## Light and camera grammar
- **Motivated sources:** windows, lamps and screens, which also tell the time of day and the mood.
- **A rise or pull-back** reveals scale: the one becomes the many.
- **A push-in** concentrates: a decision, a signature, a number.
- **Following** carries the thread between worlds. A **hold** is for reading and feeling.
- **Bookends:** return to the opening place, changed, to land a consequence (the same street, the same table, the
  same light, now off).

## Type
- Fraunces for documents, headlines and key figures.
- Inter for labels, data and captions (the approved caption style).
- Key text is at least 40 px at 1080p, and a label is seven words or fewer: readable on a phone.

## Transitions we like (invent more)
- A match cut on shape (a folder edge becomes a building edge; a ticker line becomes a map route).
- Follow the object out of frame.
- Paper to diagram: a printed line becomes a chart's axis.
- A map zooms down into a street, and a street rises into a map.

## Avoid by default
- Floating cards as the main visual.
- Literal objects in empty spaces.
- Decorative motion.
- Repeated two-shots.
- Unmotivated cuts.
- A previous episode's signature device (see `used_devices.json`).
