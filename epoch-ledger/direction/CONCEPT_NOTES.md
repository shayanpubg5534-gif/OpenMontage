# Concept notes: visual strategies for recurring financial ideas (references, not templates)

Each entry gives several possible strategies, what any image must not imply, and where it has been used. Invent new
strategies freely, and log what you use in `used_devices.json` (metaphors and signatures) so we do not repeat
ourselves.

## Credit creation (banks create deposits by lending)
- **Strategies:**
  - the double-entry moment: one keystroke, two lines on the bank's balance sheet;
  - the borrower's balance appearing out of nothing on their phone, while the vault stays sealed (P11);
  - a town's deposits rising after a lending wave.
- **Must not imply:** that banks lend out existing deposits one-for-one, or that money is printed as cash.
- **Used:** `how-banks-create-money` (double-entry ink line), P11 test (sealed vault).

## Securitisation (originate to distribute)
- **The four stages; never merge them:**
  1. **Origination:** a lender makes a loan to a person.
  2. **Pooling:** many loans are sold into a pool (a trust or SPV).
  3. **Issuance:** securities are issued against the pool and sold to investors.
  4. **Distribution and loss allocation:** borrowers' payments flow through the servicer to investors by priority
     (the waterfall). Losses are absorbed from the most junior tranche upward.
- **Strategies:**
  - follow one family's loan through a street, a back office and a trading room;
  - tranches as floors of a building, with payments filling from the top and losses flooding from the basement;
  - a map of a town whose loans converge into one pool.
- **Used:** P13 pilot (the town's loans pooled; the security as a building on the pool, paid from the top, flooded from
  the bottom). An example of the method, not the default picture for securitisation.
- **Must not imply:**
  - that the pool is the security;
  - that every missed payment destroys senior securities (they lose only when losses exceed the subordination below
    them; in 2007–08 many senior pieces were downgraded and fell in market value as expected losses rose);
  - that lenders kept no risk (warehouse lines, repurchase obligations and retained pieces kept some risk with them;
    many originators failed in 2007).

## Leverage
- **Strategies:**
  - a small slice of one's own money holding up a tall stack of borrowed money, which topples on a small fall;
  - a see-saw with the fulcrum set by the leverage ratio.
- **Must not imply:** that losses scale with anything other than the asset move times the leverage (show the
  arithmetic).

## Contagion and interconnection
- **Strategies:**
  - a network of institutions as lit windows on a night map, going dark in sequence;
  - a ledger of claims where one missing line breaks the next.
- **Must not imply:** a simple chain when the real story was a sudden freeze of trust (for example, interbank
  lending).

## Inflation
- **Strategies:**
  - the same basket of shopping across years, with prices changing on the labels;
  - a banknote's buying power as the shrinking portion of a fixed basket.
- **Must not imply:** a single cause. Show the episode's argued cause, and say so when it is contested.

## Bank runs and liquidity
- **Strategies:**
  - a queue outside a branch against a cutaway of the vault, where most deposits are loans, not cash;
  - a phone-era run as a counter of withdrawals per hour.
- **Must not imply:** that the bank had "no money": it was illiquid, which is not the same as insolvent, unless the
  story is about insolvency.

## Bubbles and manias
- **Strategies:**
  - the crowd as the market, with prices on placards;
  - an asset's price chart drawn as the outline of the crowd's skyline;
  - period newspapers stacking up.
- **Must not imply:** hindsight certainty. Show what people could reasonably believe at the time.
