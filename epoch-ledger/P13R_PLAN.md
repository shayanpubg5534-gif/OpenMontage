## 1. The refined narration (≈ 74 s spoken; the film runs 78 s with a closing hold)

| # | Narration | Pace | Seconds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | In 2006, a family signs a mortgage on their first home. The monthly payment takes almost everything they earn. | character | 0–8.5 |
| 2 | Their lender doesn't keep the loan. It sells it on. Lenders across the country do the same, and thousands of mortgages are gathered into a single pool. | diagram | 8.5–18.4 |
| 3 | On top of that pool, a new security is issued. In this simplified example, it has three layers, called tranches. Each month, the families' payments flow in. The top layer, the senior tranche, is paid first. The layers below are paid only after it. | mechanism | 18.4–35.4 |
| 4 | Pension funds, banks and insurers around the world buy these layers for their steady income. | diagram | 35.4–41.6 |
| 5 | Then house prices fall. Families who can't keep up, and can't sell for enough to repay, … | illustration | 41.6–46.0 |
| 6 | …stop paying. | emotional | 46.0–50.0 |
| 7 | Now the losses rise from the bottom. The lowest layer is wiped out first, then the next. The senior layer is still being paid, but its rating is cut, and its market value falls. In the real crisis, many senior securities lost money too. | mechanism | 50.0–64.6 |
| 8 | In September 2008, Lehman Brothers, heavily invested in mortgage-related assets, files for bankruptcy. | emotional | 64.6–78.0 |

**Accuracy:**
- The six stages are each named separately:
  - origination ("signs a mortgage");
  - the loan sale ("doesn't keep the loan… sells it on");
  - pooling ("gathered into a single pool");
  - issuance ("a new security is issued");
  - payment priority ("paid first… only after it");
  - loss allocation ("rise from the bottom… wiped out first, then the next").
- The tranche example is explicitly **simplified**, in the narration and in an on-screen tag.
- The narration states that **in the real crisis many senior securities lost money too**. The senior floors staying dry
  is the example's subordination at work, not a claim about every senior security.
- Lehman's failure is tied to its own heavy investment in mortgage-related assets, not drawn as a simple chain.
- **Wording:** natural pace (about 2.4 words per second), no filler, and no repeated ideas apart from the deliberate
  caveat.
- **Kokoro note:** its audio check flagged "families'" heard as "family's". They sound identical, so it is acceptable.

## 2. The composition plan: 10 compositions in 78 s (P13: 12 in 30 s)

Each financial idea is **one developing composition**. Motion within it is calm while the viewer reads. Labels arrive
just after the visual they name and stay up.

|---|---|---|---|

| S2 | 5.0–9.9 | illustration | Cut on action to the table from above: the pen finishes the signature. On "takes", **one** bar: payment against income. "little left" on "everything". On "lender" the camera rises off the papers. |
| S3 | 9.9–18.4 | diagram | **One map.** The table from above dissolves into the town from above: their house is gold, and their line runs to "their lender". On "thousands" the pool fills; "thousands of loans" stays. |
| S4 | 18.4–35.4 | mechanism | **One building, 17 s.** Match cut: the pool becomes the plinth. On "security" floors rise as the camera lifts. "Simplified example" tag. On "top" the camera tilts up. On "senior" the top floors light, with "Payments: senior paid first". On "layers below": "then mezzanine, then equity", and they light. |

### Remaining findings (my judgement, stated rather than hidden)
1. **Load above budget (1.8–2.2×) in three long stretches.**
   - Even P13R's calmest windows sit near the budget: one composition change, three to five staged reveals, one
     short label, and the captions (which alone account for about 25 % of the budget).
   - I set the budgets before any film existed, so they may be strict. I have not lowered them.
   - Your first viewing of the P13R render will calibrate them. If it reads comfortably, I will propose evidence-based
     budgets for approval. If not, the plan is simplified further.
2. **Four compositions in 37–55 s** (buyers → prices fall → the family → the losses). This is the story's turn. Each
   composition there is one simple image held 4–6 s, so I recommend keeping it. If it feels hurried on the render, the
   town and the kitchen can share one composition: the price line drawn over the night street through their window.
3. **Two other windows** (9–19 s and 60–74 s) are at 1.0–1.3× the composition budget. Each is one motivated change
   (the dissolve to the map; the cut to the newspaper insert).
