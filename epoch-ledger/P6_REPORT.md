# P6 — Mature stylised character prototype (visual approval gate)

**Status:** a bounded visual-development experiment. Experimental and opt-in; nothing is promoted.
- Rig v4, `render.py`, HyperFrames, the production defaults and the P5 renderer are unchanged.
- No purchases, subscriptions, GPU rental or artists.
- No animation was rendered and no rigging was done. The shading-only approach was not repeated.

**Approvals recorded:**
- P5's continuous-session renderer is the preferred experimental path for cinematic 3D shots; the live-canvas renderer
  stays the fallback.
- P5's caching, resumable rendering, deterministic shadow fix and colour consistency are approved.
- The narration and dialogue standard (commit `f3b1774`) is preserved. The P6 bank still has no actors and no speech.

Date: 2026-11-27.

**Verdict:**
- The experiment produced a measurably **mature adult**:
  - 7.4–7.7 heads tall
  - adult shoulders
  - moderate eyes
  - a defined jaw and chin
- With the available tools it did **not** reach the **premium stylised cinematic look**. Skin, hair and brows still read
  as a smooth, sculpted game avatar rather than designed, illustrated characters (§5).
- This is not presented as a production character.

## 2. What was built (all geometry, all deterministic)

`epoch-ledger/charlab/p6/build_p6.py VARIANT OUT.blend`:
- Runs in 12–15 s per variant.
- Rebuilding gives identical meshes (vertex hash `d8c97639c8a56d9b`, checked twice).

1. **Mature base**, shared by every variant: database morphs toward a 35–45-year-old man. The values were chosen from a
   rendered morph study:
   - age 0.45, jaw 2.0, gonial angle 0.6, zygomatic 1.0, chin width 0.7
   - shoulder width 1.8 and blade 2.8
   - trapezius, neck girth, a little fat
   - brow ridge slightly reduced
   - Small deterministic **facial asymmetry**: left brow +1 mm, right mouth corner and jaw a little fuller.
2. **Geometric stylisation:** controlled operators on the mesh, not on shading.
   - **Relief simplification:** Laplacian low-pass of small soft-tissue relief, stronger on the face.
   - **Plane accentuation:** band-pass "unsharp masking" of the head's mid-frequency forms, so the jaw line, cheekbones,
     brow and nose bridge become crisper planes. It is weighted to the face.
   - **Head proportion:** a scale about the neck with a smooth falloff (eyes never scaled alone).
   - The CharMorph `Generic_Stylized` morph is used in B and C.
3. **Pose:**
   - The arms are lowered from the base A-pose with forearm pronation (palms to the thighs), and the fingers are relaxed
     about the knuckle line.
   - This uses linear-blend skinning with Reom's own Rigify weights and joints, the same data a real rig would use.
4. **Wardrobe:** separate meshes on the posed body.
   - **Shirt:**
     - An offset shell with welded seams, low-pass and collision passes.
     - A front drape envelope, so the shirt hangs from the chest instead of following it.
     - Tuck blousing, elbow and armpit folds.
     - A neckline drawn in to a modelled collar: stand and pointed leaf.
     - A four-in-hand tie and knot.
   - **Trousers:**
     - Hips and thighs are a shell; each leg is lofted as clean rings hanging straight to a hem that rests on the shoe,
       shorter at the front.
     - The break, folds behind the knee and a pressed crease.
   - **Belt and buckle.**
   - **Derby shoes:** a procedural last from the foot's measured outline, with no toes, a longer toe and a welted sole.
5. **Mesh hair** (no particles):
   - A shell over the base's own scalp mask, with the hairline lowered and smoothed.
   - Geodesic feathering to a tapered hairline.
   - A side part, with flow-aligned clumps, strands and fine strands.
6. **Brows:** sculpted mesh ribbons along a softened brow curve, fuller at the head and fine at the tail.
7. **Materials:**
   - Restrained painted skin: warm cheeks, nose and ears; a lip tint from the base's mask; a light beard shadow from its
     mask. No pore or photo textures.
   - Cloth and hair darken in folds and cavities.

## 3. Measured proportions vs the approved standard

Definitions are identical to `convert/measure_props.py`, measured on the posed body without clothes or hair.

| | Raw Reom | **A** | **B** | **C** | Standard |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Height | 1.825 m | 1.880 m | 1.876 m | 1.874 m | — |
| Head height | 0.246 m | 0.254 m | 0.250 m | 0.242 m | — |
| **Heads tall** | 7.41 | **7.41 ✓** | **7.51 ✓** | **7.74 ✓** | 7.0–8.0, 7.5 preferred |
| **Shoulders** (biacromial ÷ head height) | 1.31 ✗ | **1.50 ✓** (on the limit) | **1.53 ✓** | **1.59 ✓** | men ≥ 1.5 (1.6–1.8 typical) |
| **Eye opening ÷ head width** | 16.4% | **16.9% ✓** | **17.4% ✓** | **18.6% ✓** | ≤ 24% |
| Neck / trapezius | — | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | Integrated (visual) |
| Face: jaw, chin, nose, cheekbones | Young, soft | ✓ adult | ✓ adult | ✓ adult, crisper planes | Visual |
| Hands | 5 fingers | ✓ relaxed | ✓ | ✓ | Visual |

**Every row of §1 of `CHARACTER_STANDARD.md` passes for A, B and C.** A's shoulders sit exactly on the limit.

The measurements also caught two problems that were then fixed:
- **Age and fat morphs:** at higher values (age 0.7, fat 0.22) they soften the jaw and lower the measured chin, which
  made the head too tall (6.88 heads). They were reduced.
- **Shoulders:** the base's shoulders started at 1.31 head heights, below the minimum for men. The shoulder morphs
  were raised.

## 4. Contextual still

`bank_still.png` is variant B, exported by `p6/export_glb.py` (skin paint baked to a 2,048 px texture). It is
rendered in the **real P4 cinematic bank set in the three.js runtime** (`cinematicVariant p4`, `cin_cinematic`
profile, haze, lens blur), with the same lighting as P4.

Runtime cost: the GLB is **252k triangles** with one texture, at the standard's ~250k budget.

What the runtime still does **not** carry over from Cycles:
- the cavity tint on cloth and hair;
- the eye cornea, which is dropped;
- subsurface and sheen, which glTF renders far too bright in three.js.

The character is static: there is no skeleton, so it does not breathe, blink or look.

**What the still shows** (full figure, 44° lens, standing in front of the desk):
- The character sits correctly in the set's light and scale.
- Problems:
  - The padded sleeve heads.
  - The shirt reads flatter and whiter than in Cycles.
  - The fingernails come out black, a bake artefact on the nail UV islands.
  - The face lacks modelling under the set's soft light.

One opt-in runtime change was needed: a static GLB prop can now keep its own materials with `keepMaterials: true`.
Every other demo keeps the old shading (tested).

## 5. Artistic assessment (the gate criterion)

**What improved over P5 and M5:**
- Adult proportions, measured.
- A man's jaw, chin, nose and brow.
- Moderate eyes.
- A believable silhouette in a shirt, tie, trousers, belt and shoes.
- A pointed collar and tie that read correctly at documentary distances.
- Hair that is mesh, groomed and parted, not particles.
- Nothing child-like: no large head or eyes, no infant cheeks, no cartoon brows.

**What still fails the premium stylised standard**, visible on every sheet:
- **Surface and form design:** the face is anatomically plausible but undesigned. The forms are smooth and generic
  ("mannequin" / game avatar). Plane accentuation sharpens them but does not create the deliberate simplification and
  appeal an artist gives a stylised head; at C it starts to create unwanted creases (upper lip).
- **Hair:** procedural flow grooves read as a sculpted helmet with ripples, not designed locks with silhouette breakup.
- **Brows and eyes:**
  - The brows are clean but read as applied ribbons.
  - The eyes have no modelled lashes or lid thickness.
  - The irises are flat colour.
- **Materials:** painted skin without texture reads as plastic under strong light. The illustrated quality we want
  (painted gradients, edge treatment, designed specular) needs texture painting or a dedicated shader; Cycles' simple
  principled materials do not supply it.
- **Wardrobe:**
  - The folds are plausible but generic.
  - The shoulders and sleeve heads look padded (they follow Reom's deltoids).
  - The shirt still shows some chest relief.
  - A tailor-quality fit would need cloth simulation on a properly cut pattern, or a modelled garment.
- **Strongest candidate:**
  - **B** gives the best balance.
  - **A** is the most natural but the plainest.
  - **C**'s stronger accentuation starts to introduce artefacts rather than style.

**Conclusion:** controlled geometric stylisation with Blender Python fixes **proportions, maturity and construction**
(rig data, wardrobe and hair as separate reusable meshes, measured standards). It does **not** produce the **designed,
premium stylised look**. That gap is form design, hair design and material painting, which is artist work. The
remaining defects are not hidden by the camera, shadows or clothing.

## 6. Technical feasibility of this base (what was tested vs not)

| Capability | Status | Evidence / what is missing |
|---|---|---|
| Body rigging, natural movement | **Partly shown** | The base ships Rigify joints and weights. P6 posed arms, forearms and fingers with them (LBS). No armature was generated and no motion tested |
| Walking, sitting, standing | **Not tested** | Needs the generated Rigify rig retargeted to our mocap clips (as for Snow in P3), then corrective shapes for shoulders and hips |
| Hand gestures, object interaction | **Partly shown** | Finger chains exist and curl correctly. Our gesture library and prop sockets are not mapped |
| Facial expressions, eye movements | **Data present, not tested** | 51 facial blendshapes (blinks, look directions, jaw, mouth, brows, cheeks). Not mapped to our expression units, not rendered |
| Optional lip sync (scripted dialogue only) | **Data present, not tested** | `jawOpen`, `mouthClose`, funnel, pucker and similar shapes could drive our viseme units. Disabled by default per the narration standard |
| Reusable hair and wardrobe | **Shown (static)** | Separate meshes generated from the body; they adapt to every variant and would follow other identities. Skinning them needs weight transfer (standard, not done) |
| Distinct adult identities | **Shown in principle** | 117 anatomical and ethnicity morphs; three variants built from one script. Not a cast yet |
| Male and female | **Not tested** | Antonia (CC-BY 3.0) uses the same system; its wardrobe would need women's garments |
| Ages and body types | **Partly shown** | Age, fat and muscle morphs exist; at higher age values the jaw softens (§3) |
| Historical clothing | **Not tested** | The garment builders are parametric (collar shape, trouser cut). Period cuts are design work |
| Conversion into our runtime | **Static only** | A static GLB renders in the P4 set (252k triangles). A skinned conversion needs a converter profile for Rigify names (about a day, as for Snow) |
| Licence | **Verified** | CC-BY for the asset (version to confirm); GPL code not used; our additions are our own |

## 7. Limitations
- **Not production-ready:** the appearance gate is not met (§5).
- The bank still loses Cycles-only material effects (§4).
- Render-level mesh is about 980k triangles in Cycles (subdivision); the runtime export is about 252k.
- **Shoulder margin:** A sits exactly on the 1.5 head-height minimum; reducing the padded look further pushes it below.
- **C's artefacts:** the band-pass accent creates small creases (upper lip, brow) at high strength.

## 8. Recommendation: is further development justified?

**Further free, automatic development is not justified.** Another round of procedural tuning would polish the same
mannequin.

**This base and pipeline are worth keeping as the technical foundation:**
- Reom's licence, anatomy, rig data and face shapes.
- Our measured proportions, garment and hair builders, and GLB path.
- These are a sound target for the cast system you described: one base, many identities, reusable wardrobe.

**The step that changes the look is an artist** (your decision; nothing was contacted). The artist would make a **stylised
style kit on this base**:
- a sculpted stylised head and body target set (as Reom morphs, so identities stay parametric);
- designed hair pieces;
- a painted skin and cloth material set;
- tailored wardrobe meshes.

Our pipeline would then produce the variants, measure them and fit them to the runtime.

**Alternative:** the Mikassa membership option from P5, for a ready-made premium adult. That gives one character, not a
system.
